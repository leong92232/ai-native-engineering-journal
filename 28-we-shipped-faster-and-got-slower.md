# We Shipped Faster and Got Slower

AI is fast. That part isn't in question.

The problem sits somewhere else — in how much AI actually understands about what we're building, versus how much a real solution actually needs.

A working solution isn't one idea. It's the accumulated understanding of a team — years of context, tradeoffs, exceptions, things that got tried and abandoned, things that broke once and were never done that way again. If you tried to write all of that down, it'd be a thick book. Nobody writes it down. It lives in the team.

Then someone gives AI one line. Build this.

AI builds it. Fast, confident, plausible. But that one line was never the whole book. It was a summary of a summary. So AI fills the rest in on its own — reasonably, most of the time, but not with the same context the team is carrying. That's not a flaw in the AI. It's just math. One line in, a thick book of missing context, and AI has to guess at what's not there.

Here's where the speed quietly disappears.

The output looks done. Someone on the team reads it, and now has to figure out: does this actually match what we needed, or does it just look like it does? That's not a quick check. That's re-deriving the intent from the output, holding it against everything the team knows that never made it into the one-line request, and finding the gaps one at a time.

That step — verify, compare, correct — takes real time. Sometimes more time than it would have taken a person to just build it the slower way, holding the full context the whole way through, instead of building fast and reconciling after.

So the team ships faster. And the team gets slower. Both are true, and they're not in tension — they're the same event, measured at two different points in the process.

This isn't an argument against AI speed. It's a warning about where the cost moves. It doesn't disappear when AI builds fast. It moves downstream, into the verification step, and it often lands heavier than it would have upfront — because now it's not "build with context," it's "build blind, then reconstruct the context after the fact to check the work."

The fix isn't slowing AI down. It's making sure more of that thick, unwritten book actually makes it into the request before AI starts — because every piece of context you don't front-load, someone has to pay for on the back end, at a worse exchange rate.

Fast in, slow to trust. That's the trade nobody prices in until the team's already paying it.
