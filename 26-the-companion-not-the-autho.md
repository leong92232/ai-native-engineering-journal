# The Companion, Not the Author

Day 6 was about asking a leading question and getting the answer I wanted. This one's a different trap, and it took me longer to notice.

It's not when I ask "is this good, right?" It's when I open AI with nothing yet. No idea, no direction, just "what should I do here."

That's a different failure than confirmation bias. Nobody's leading anybody. The question is honest. But when there's nothing in my head to weigh the answer against, whatever comes back — fluent, reasonable, confident — just becomes my idea by default. Not because I agreed with it. Because there was no "I" there yet to disagree.

I noticed it happen with small things first. A message I didn't know how to word. A structure I hadn't thought through. I'd ask, get something reasonable, use it, move on — and a week later if someone asked me why I did it that way, I didn't really have an answer beyond "that's what came out."

That's the line I try to hold now, and it's got nothing to do with how I phrase the question:

> Did I have a position before I asked, or did I ask because I had none?

Sharpening an idea I already hold — that's companion work. AI make it better, faster, cleaner. Fine.

Generating the idea from a blank page and letting it become mine because it arrived first — that's not companion work anymore. That's letting AI author it, quietly, without either of us noticing the moment it happened.

So my practice: before I open AI, force myself to hold something, even something rough, even something I'm not sure about. A one-line guess. A direction, wrong or not. Then bring AI in against that. Not into an empty room.

The idea still has to exist before I ask. Otherwise there was never really a "mine" to protect.
