# The Fun Part Is Asking "What's Still Pending?"

What's pending? That's a question worth asking myself constantly, and lately I ask AI too.

It started as a small habit. End of day, or start of one, I'd ask: check the code, tell me what's still open, what's broken, what got left behind. Not a big process. Just a question.

I noticed I liked asking it. That surprised me a little — most inventory tasks feel like chores. This one didn't. Because the answer usually came back fast, and it usually surfaced something I'd have taken a week to notice on my own, if I noticed at all.

That's the actual value. Not that AI is smarter about my system than I am. It's that AI doesn't get tired of the checklist. I do. A pending bug, a half-finished migration, an unverified script sitting quietly in production — none of these are hidden. They're all sitting right there, in commit messages, in half-written notes, in a pending-actions list I wrote myself and then stopped reading. They're findable. They just require someone to go looking, and looking is the thing that never survives a busy week.

So I ask. And most days, it's useful — a real gap gets named before it turns into a real problem.

But I don't treat the answer as final, and that matters more than the habit itself.

Here's why: humans miss things. That's not news. But AI misses things too, differently, and for its own reasons. It can miss context that never made it into a comment. It can miss a conversation that happened in a meeting instead of a commit. It can miss the informal "we agreed to postpone this" that lives only in someone's memory. Ask two ways, get two different pictures. Ask the same way twice, sometimes still get two different pictures.

So the practice isn't "ask AI, done." It's "ask AI, then confirm." The question surfaces candidates. It doesn't close the loop by itself. If AI flags five things pending, I still walk through them — is this actually still open, did we already handle it somewhere AI can't see, is this a miscommunication rather than a gap. Some of what comes back is noise. Some of it is the exact thing that would've slipped past me for another month.

That combination — AI's persistence, my judgment — is what makes the habit work. Neither one alone is enough. AI without a human checking will confidently report gaps that aren't real, or miss ones that are only visible outside the code. A human without the habit just... forgets, the way busy weeks make everyone forget.

The fun part isn't finding AI smart. It's that the backlog never gets a real chance to go invisible anymore. Something that used to require deliberate, effortful review — sitting down, reading through everything, holding it all in your head at once — became a five-minute habit instead.

What's pending? Ask often. Trust the question more than the answer.
